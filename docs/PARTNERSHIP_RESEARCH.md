# CLICKMINT — Quality Partnership Research (winning a BIG/first-class partner with <700 subs)

**Problem you asked:** I have a channel close to (or under) 700 subscribers. I want to partner
with a genuinely high-quality, *larger* channel. But how do I (1) find and filter the quality
ones, (2) know what *types* of channels exist, (3) understand what value/leverage I can offer,
and (4) agree on a contract they'll actually accept?

**Core truth this whole document stands on:**
> A bigger channel partners with you because of **value and audience fit**, not your subscriber
> count. A channel with 630 engaged subscribers is a *better* partner than one with 10,000
> dead ones — that's the entire basis of your leverage.

---

## PART 1 — The 3-part compatibility filter (this is the master key)

The single most useful framework — it comes from cross-promotion research in adjacent media
(podcast-to-podcast swaps) and maps perfectly to Telegram. **All three must pass.** If you only
chase "big" and skip these, you waste every outreach.

| Test | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Audience overlap** | Their members could plausibly benefit from yours (and vice-versa) | If no overlap, nobody joins from the swap |
| **2. Content adjacency** | *Related but NOT competing* topics — your AI/crypto/airdrop/safety sits **alongside** their AI/crypto/tools/education | Coverage of the exact same ground cannibalizes a shared audience → kills the deal |
| **3. Comparable (or acceptable) size** | Similar size is easiest; for a bigger channel you need the size gap bridged with **extra value** | A huge size gap usually means they gain nothing from you unless you give more |

> **The secret for a channel your size:** the deciding factor between "big, no" and "quality,
> yes" is **content adjacency**. A smaller, highly-engaged, *complementary* channel beats a big,
> general, low-engagement one — for both of you.

### A quality-checker (the scorecard) — run this before you contact anyone
| Check | Pass? |
|---|---|
| Public, active, posts consistently (≥ a few times/week) | ☐ |
| **Views ÷ subscribers is high** (40–60%+, small channels often 60–85%) | ☐ |
| Real reactions/comments (not 90% bots) | ☐ |
| Topic is **adjacent** to yours (AI, crypto, web3, tools, education, wallets, TON) — not identical | ☐ |
| Audience is English/global (matches yours) | ☐ |
| Content you'd be **proud** to be seen next to | ☐ |
| No scam/guaranteed-profit/impersonation content | ☐ |

## PART 2 — Where to find & how to filter

### Discovery sources
| Tool | What it gives you | Price | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TGStat** | Channel catalog: topic, subscribers, growth, engagement, activity | Free tier + paid | The main discovery + the engagement numbers you need |
| **Telemetr** | Similar stats + analytics | Free/paid | Cross-checking |
| **Comboot / Combot directory** | Niche channel lists + community analytics | Free | Finding candidates |
| **Telega.io** | Marketplace (for paid, but its channel list is useful) | Paid | Later, once you consider paid placements |
| **Telegram Suggest/Similar Channels** | Telegram's own "similar channels" on any channel profile | Free | Find who Telegram thinks is adjacent — powerful hint |
| **Telegram Search** (+ niche groups you're already in) | Manual, organic | Free | The people who already engage with you |

### The key: use ENGAGEMENT rate, not subscriber count, as your filter
Because subscriber count is a vanity metric, and you already understand this. Use the
**benchmark ratios** (from SMM Plus's 2026 engagement study):

| Channel size | Healthy view % (views ÷ subs) | Strong reaction % |
|---|---|---|
| **Under 1,000** | **60 – 85%** | **10 – 20%** |
| 1K – 10K | 40 – 65% | 5 – 15% |
| 10K – 50K | 30 – 55% | 3 – 10% |
| 50K+ | 20 – 50% | 2 – 6% |

**This is your biggest insight.** Small channels (like yours at ~630) naturally have the
**highest view + reaction rates** of any size. So:

- A **630-sub channel with 60%+ views** is a *better* promotional asset than a 10K channel
  with 25% views.
- **Lead with your engagement ratio**, not your subscriber count. That's real leverage.

### Filtering recipe
1. Search on TGStat by topic (crypto/airdrop/AI/tools/web3/TON/wallet-safety/education).
2. Set subscriber range **700 – 5,000** (start) — these are the ones that will say yes and give
   you real reach. Aim for **content adjacency**, not identical niche.
3. Sort by **engagement rate**, not subs. Use the benchmarks above.
4. Shortlist the top ~30 and run the quality scorecard (Part 1).
5. Prefer channels **growing** (rising engagement) over flat ones — they're hungry for
   partners and their audience is active.

## PART 3 — The types of channels (and which accept *your* value)

Different formats have different needs. Matching your offer to their format is what makes
them say yes.

### By format / function
| Type | What they do | What they WANT | Will they accept your value? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **News/announcement** (update channel) | Broadcast updates | Fresh, useful, consistent content | ✅ high — they need content |
| **Education/guides** (your sweet spot) | Teach beginners | Guest guides, explainers, checklists | ✅ **highest** — you make resources |
| **Curated/aggregator** | "Top tools/airdrops this week" | Lists, verified info, roundups | ✅ high — you contribute value |
| **AI-tools/tech** | Reviews, tools, workflows | Tool roundups, use-cases | ✅ high |
| **Community-centered** (+group) | Discussion, help, links | Content that sparks discussion | ✅ high |
| **Deals/drops/airdrop** | Opportunities, deadlines | Verified, safe, deadline content | ✅ high (adjacent, be safe-first) |
| **Personal brand / creator** | The owner's voice | Guest spots, credibility, collabs | ⚠️ medium — needs brand fit |
| **Trading/signals** (be careful) | Price/signal | — | ❌ low — competing + risky; avoid on safety reasons |
| **Opportunity-scalping/hype** | Guaranteed-returns spam | — | ❌ never — hurts CLICKMINT credibility |

### Your best partner categories (where your value is highest)
1. **AI-tools / AI x crypto channels** — you deliver verified AI-tool guides + crypto safety.
2. **Crypto-education / beginner channels** — you deliver beginner guides, checklists, "confirmed vs potential" explainers.
3. **Airdrop/testnet aggregators** (that are safety-minded) — you deliver verified + scam-alerts.
4. **Web3/TON community channels** — you deliver ecosystem quest/testnet content.
5. **Wallet-safety / security channels** — you deliver scam alerts + safety checklists.

## PART 4 — Your LEVERAGE with <700 subs (this is what actually wins)

You keep asking "what leverage do I have?" — the honest answer is **several, and most channel
owners underrate them.** Here's your real asset list:

### 1. Your engagement ratio (your strongest card)
You're in the **60–85% view-rate band**. Big channels are usually in the 30–45% band. You can
offer a **measured, converting audience** — which a big channel cannot easily get from a
"click == subscriber" buy.

### 2. You create value content (not just "join me")
Most small channels only ask. You can **give** — scannable guides, checklists, "confirmed vs
potential" explainers, AI-tool roundups, deadline calendars. A partner posts *your* curated
resource and looks like an expert to their audience. **This is the #1 thing a channel owner
wants from a cross-post** — content their audience actually appreciates.

### 3. Your safety/verification positioning
In crypto/airdrop (scam-heavy), a channel that's *trusted, verified, anti-scam* is genuinely
valuable. It complements rather than competes, and protects their audience.

### 4. Your community group (the value multiplier)
You have a linked discussion group. Offering a partner a **joint Link-Check/session, a Q&A, or a
guest spot in your group** is value they can't get elsewhere.

### 5. Asymmetric / favorable terms (level the size gap)
Given a bigger channel, **offer TWO of yours for ONE of theirs.**
> From the research: "Share their subscriber count relative to yours and propose asymmetric
> deals if needed — e.g. you'll share twice if their audience is larger."
This is the honest way a small player wins a bigger yes. It costs you nothing except effort,
and it makes the size gap disappear.

### 6. Cross-platform repurposing (your leverage is the info, not the channel)
You can also offer a partner **differentiated content** they can republish across their
platforms, or co-create a joint resource, or feature them in your own roundup.

## PART 5 — The contract / terms (what you both agree on)

Make it explicit. This protects you, and it *shows* them you're professional. Nothing here is
legal legalese — it's a short mutual understanding that makes the deal clean.

### The deal offer (pick the format you're proposing)
| Deal type | What each does |
|---|---|
| **Cross-post / shoutout swap** | Each posts a short card about the other (once) |
| **Content swap** | You give a ready-to-publish resource; they publish it + credit you; they give something back |
| **Recurring partner** | Weekly/mutual mentions or a recurring roundup slot |
| **Guest content** | You write a guide/interview for their channel (their audience, your name) |
| **Joint resource / co-branded list** | You co-create a guide/bundle both publish; both get exposure |

### The terms to agree on (a checklist to share)
1. **Exactly what each posts** — a ready-to-paste card is best (you write both, so it's consistent).
2. **Date + time (UTC)** — and how long it stays pinned (e.g. 24–48h).
3. **Content class** — you agree the topic/type so neither posts junk.
4. **Attribution** — the card must include your handle, and next to theirs.
5. **Audience protection** — no posting stuff that damages either reputation (no guaranteed-profit, no scam).
6. **How it's measured** — typical metric: subscribers gained + retention after 30 days. For a
   bigger partner, offer a measurable win: "X engaged joins" or a tracked invite link.
7. **Volume** — how many posts total (1, 2–5, or recurring).
8. **Renewal** — if it worked, run it again; if not, stop cleanly.
9. **Reciprocity for the size gap** — if they're bigger, *you* do more (2:1), clearly stated.
10. **No exclusivity** — neither is locked in; both free to partner elsewhere.
11. **Standards** — both keep real engagement; no bought members/views.

### The "ready-to-paste card" is your strongest negotiating tool
Give them a card that's done: title, 3–5 bullet value points, branding, and their CTA. It takes
them 30 seconds to post. **Owners say yes faster when the work is already done.**

## PART 6 — The actual outreach (how to frame it for a bigger/quality channel)

The research is unambiguous: **vague "let's collaborate" messages go unanswered.** Reference
something *specific* about their channel, state the overlap plainly, give your real (engagement)
stats, and propose a concrete format + timeline.

### The "quality → adjacent → value-first" pitch
```
Hey [Name],

I follow [Channel] — the [specific post/topic] you did on [X] was genuinely sharp, and
I think our audiences sit right next to each other.

I run CLICKMINT (@ClickMintHQ) — a verified AI/crypto/airdrop research channel. We're
small (~630 subs) but our posts pull a ~[60–80]% view rate, and we specialize in
safety/verified guides — so I think your [audience/wallet-safety/AI-tools] readers
would actually find us valuable, not just more airdrop spam.

Here's a proposal I think is fair:
• You post one card about CLICKMINT (I'll write it, ready to paste, ~2 lines).
• I'll post TWO cards about your channel (2:1, since you're bigger).
• It goes live [date] at [UTC time], pinned 24h.

No exclusivity, no money, and if it doesn't help you we just don't repeat it.
Want to try one?

Best, [Name]
```

### Why this works
- **Specific, not generic.** References their actual content → not a spam blast.
- **Your engagement, not your size.** Positions you as a real asset.
- **2:1 asymmetry.** Removes the "what do I get from you?" objection.
- **Concrete + low-risk.** Date/time/pinned/terms all stated; "no exclusivity, stop if it fails."

## PART 7 — The realistic trap (so you don't waste energy)

**Chasing the enormous channels is almost always a dead end.** The research is consistent:
the channels most likely to accept, and to *actually* bring you value, are **similar or
slightly-larger channels (size-comparable)** — not the 100K giants. A giant gains ~nothing from
you, won't reply, and if they do, they'll treat you as a paid placement.

**The smart strategy with <700 subs:**
- **Tier 1 (main effort):** high-engagement channels **700 – 2,500 subs**, content-adjacent.
  These are your growth engine and they say yes. 
- **Tier 2 (stretch):** 2,500 – 5,000 high-engagement, adjacent. Use 2:1 + your content value.
- **Tier 3 (occasional, not a goal):** 5,000+ — only if there's a clear content or
  complementary fit, and only with your strongest value offer.
- **Advance as you grow.** Every +100 subs you gain makes the next (bigger) yes easier. Your
  leverage compounds — this is the whole reason the bot/tracker exists.

## Final principle

> A bigger channel says yes to **a specific, well-framed offer** from a **small but genuinely
> high-engagement, complementary** channel — never to a vague "let's collab" from a stranger.
> Your leverage at <700 subs is your **engagement ratio** + the **value content you produce** +
> a **favorable/2:1 deal**. Target adjacent, high-engagement, 700–2.5K channels first; your
> leverage compounds as you grow.
